Question 1
L1-02 (Selection) (2013 EQE)	(8 points)


A British company A intends to file together with Mr. Surat as joint applicants a PCT application at the Patent Office of India. Mr. Surat is a national and resident of India. 


a) As no agent is required before the Patent Office of India acting as receiving Office, it is decided that Mr. Surat should represent the applicants during the international phase. What options are there to achieve this and what steps must be taken to this end? 

 - IN as rO requires an address for service in India - India Patent Office Annex C; AG-IP.
 - Mr Surat is resident of India - requirement fulfilled. 
 - He has right to file IA as resident and national of India - A9(1); R19(1)(i)/R19(1)(ii).
 - Joint applicants can file IA if at least one of them is entitled to file IA - R.18.3 + R19.2(i).
 - Mr Surat can either be: (1) appointed to be the common representative under R90.2(a); or (2) he can become the deemed common representative simply by virtue of the fact that he is the only Applicant entitled to file an IA at the IN rO, and is thus the first named entitled Applicant on the IA - R90.2(b).
 - No need for separate and general power of attorney in that case for Mr Surat to represent the applicants during the international phase; all applicants just need to sign the request - R. 90.4(a). 
 - However, they can also issue a PoA authorizing Mr Surat to be an agent for the IA - R90.4(b).
 - As deemed common rep. he cannot do withdrawals of IA, prio claim, demand or designations - R90bis.5



b) The applicants select the EPO as International Searching Authority (ISA) and International Preliminary Examining Authority (IPEA). For the procedure before the EPO as IPEA under Chapter II PCT the applicants decide to seek professional help. 

b1) Can they appoint an Indian agent who has the right to practice before the Patent Office of India? 
 - YES. They can appoint an Indian agent who has the right to practice before the rO, which was IN in the international phase. The right to practice before national office as rO entitles to practice before the ISA and IPEA - Art 49; R90.1(a).
 - They can appoint him by either signing the demand - R90.4(a); or signing a separate PoA - R90.4(b).

b2) Can they appoint a European professional representative?

- YES. They can appoint EP professional representative to act before the EPO - A134(1) EPC; 
- For the EPO as ISA, direct legal basis in the PCT is R.90.1(b);
- For the EPO as IPEA, direct legal basis in the PCT is R.90.1(c);
- EPO waived requirement for PoA for EPO representatives - R.90.4(d).

c) For the procedure in the European phase the applicants consider continuing without professional help. Can Mr. Wilson, employee of company A, act as a common representative before the EPO? 

- NO, he cannot.
- No representation is forced for CS residents before the EPO - A133(1) EPC
- EPO as eO applies national law under A27(7) PCT.  Requires professional representation for Euro-PCT entry in case the Applicants don't have residence in EPC CS - A133(2) EPC.
 - If one of the applicants is obliged to appoint a professional representative, this representative shall be deemed to be the common representative - R151(1) EPC.
- British company A presumably has residency or place of business in the UK, which is an EPC state. I will not make further edge-case assumptions about whether or not this British company has or has not an address in the UK. 
- Thus, in principle, they are allowed to be represented by an employee who need not be an EPO representative - A133(3) EPC. 
- However, Mr Surat is obliged to have professional representation before the EPO on Euro-PCT entry. He cannot be represented by Mr Wilson.
- Mr Surat's appointed professional rep will be deemed common rep for all applicants - R151(1) EPC
- However, if A named first and appoints professional rep, this rep will be deemed common rep for everyone - R151(1) EPC.
- Mr. Wilson, employee of company A, can act as the common representative before the EPO only if he just happens to also be a professional representive before the EPO???

Question 2
2015 Paper D1, Question 3 		(9 points) 

The German national Hans is working and living in Argentina. He is employed by Evita-Engineering (EE), which is a company constituted under Argentinean law and is based in Buenos Aires. Hans and company EE validly filed a national patent application A at the competent authority in Argentina. It is intended to file today an international patent application PCT-A in English claiming priority of application A. In the PCT request, company EE and Hans are named as applicants. 

a)  May PCT-A contain a declaration claiming the priority of application A?

- YES; PCT-A may contain a declaration claiming priority to application A - R4.10(a)
- Priority may be claimed to any validly filed national patent application from a signatory of the Paris Convention - Art8 PCT and Art4A(1) Paris and Art4A(2) Paris.
- ARG is signatory of Paris (and member of WTO) - ???
- PCT-A must contain the priority declaration in the request.


b)  May Hans and company EE file PCT-A jointly?

- YES; they may file PCT-A jointly.
- Joint applicants can file IA if at least one of them is entitled to file IA - R.18.3 + R19.2(i).
- Hans is entitled to file an IA as he is a citizen of Germany, which is a CS - A9(1) PCT
- He can file with the nO of CS of which he is a national - R19.1(a)(ii).


c)  Which of the following offices will act as the receiving Office for PCT-A? 
1)  The EPO,
2)  The government authority entrusted with the grant of patents of Germany (German Patent and Trademark Office, GPTO). 


- Competent rOs for Hans as a national of DE are: the EPO, the IB and the DPMA - AG-IP:DE - Annex B.
- However, it is specified that PCT-A is to be filed in English. 
- DPMA does not accept IAs filed in English as competent rO - AG-IP:DE - Annex C.
- EPO accepts Its filed in English as competent rO - AG-IP:EP - Annex C.
- The EPO will act as rO for PCT-A - A151 EPC & R157(1) EPC.
- Alternatively, they can file with the IB in English as IB accepts filings in any language - AG-IP:IB - Annex C.
- If filed with DPMA, the application will be considered to have been received on the date of filing by the DPMA on behalf of the IB as rO - R19.4(a)(ii) & R19.1(a)(iii)

Question 4
L2-27 (EQE - 6 points)

Today is 11 March 2025. A Spanish company filed an international application in Spanish with the EPO on 21 February 2025. The international application validly claims priority from a Spanish application filed on 21 February 2024. All necessary fees were paid to the EPO. The Spanish company received a notification from the EPO that the application had been transmitted to the International Bureau.
Why did the EPO transmit the application, what effect does this have and what has to be done next?

- It has been 19 days since filing.
- Spanish company entitled to file IA at EPO as rO - A151 EPC & R157(1) EPC.
- IA filed at EPO must be in EN/FR/DE - AG-IP:EP - Annex C & R157(2) EPC
- Otherwise put; IA must be in prescribed language of rO - A3(4)(i) & R12.1(a)
- Since the IA is not in a language prescribed by the rO, it is received by the EPO on behalf of the IB and transmitted to the IB - R19.4(a)(ii)
- Date of filing is unaffected - all fees have been duly paid - R19.4(b).
- IB will act as rO - R19.1(a)(iii)
- However, EPO will refund the fees paid to the EPO - AG-IP 6.034 & OJ 1993, 764.
- Transmittal fee, filing fee and search fee must then be paid to IB as rO within 1m of date of receipt by the IB - R14.1(c), R15.3, R16.1(f).
- Date of receipt is the date of actual receipt by IB - R19.4(c).
- 11 Mar 25 + 1m -> 11 Apr 25. Working day. No extensions under R80.5. The fees must be paid until 11 April 2025.
- If applicant supplies translation into DE/FR/EN, EPO may act as ISA - R12.3.

Question 7
L2-38 (2014 Paper D1, Question 4)		(7 points)

Mr. Smith is the sole applicant and inventor of an international application. This application, containing the prescribed request together with a separate power of attorney for an agent, was filed at the EPO as receiving Office. The request was signed by the agent. The power of attorney was not signed at all. 

a)  Is there a defect in the application in view of the requirements for signatures? 

- NO there is no defect in the IA in view of the requirements for signatures.
- Separate power of attorney must be submitted to rO or IB - R90.4(b)
- It may be waived by rO - R90.4(d) & R90.5(c)
- EPO in its capacity as rO has waived this requirement under Rule 90.4(b) - PCT-EPO Guidelines, A‑VIII, 1.13; OJ 2010, 335
- Power of attorney needs to be duly signed by the Applicant - R90.4(c)
- The power of attorney was not signed at all in this case.
- Thus, it is considered non-existent - R90.4(c)
- Hence, missing signature CANNOT be considered a defect.

b)  Who can withdraw the international application and what has to be done to this end? 

- WHO? Both Applicant and agent can withdraw the IA.
- Applicant may withdraw IA at ANY TIME prior to 30m from priority date - R90bis.1(a)
- So it doesnt matter that there is a defect in the IA on filing.
- Applicant can simply submit a notice of withdrawal under R90.bis.5
- He can also submit a duly signed separate or general power of attorney and have the agent sign the withdrawal notice as the waiver does not apply for withdrawals - R90.4(e) (also AG-IP 11.048)
- WHAT TO BE DONE?: Request for withdrawal must be filed with the EPO (rO) or IB - R90bis.1(a); and prior to 30m from priority date - R90bis.1(a)
