Question 1
C7-10 (EQE - 7.5 points) 


Professor Pivo from Prague (CZ), inventor of a revolutionary new beer-brewing process, files a Czech national application CZ-A on 6 January 2025 as a first filing, describing and claiming his brewing process. On 6 January 2026 he presents the process in detail in a lecture at a public congress in Vienna. On the same day, just before his lecture, he puts a European patent application EP-A claiming the priority of CZ-A in the letter box of the EPO's Vienna sub-office. 
EP-A is an exact translation of CZ-A into English. EP-A is transmitted to the EPO's Munich office and received there on 12 January 2026. Today is 3 March 2026. 
Discuss whether the lecture will be considered novelty destroying for EP-A. 


Timeline:
- CZ-A: 6 JAN 2025
- EP-A given to Vienna sub-office claiming prio to CZ-A: 6 JAN 2026
- Public lecture: 6 JAN 2026
- EP-A in ENG is received at Munich office on 12 JAN 2026
- TODAY: 3 MAR 2026

On the issue of trying to file at Vienna office:
- EP applications may be filed online or in person at the Hague or Munich or Berlin locations - A75(1); R2(1); R35(1). [also at national offices (A75(1)(b)) but irrelevant here].
- Applications received at the Vienna sub-office will be forwarded to one of the filing offices and will have their date of filing accorded as the date of receipt of the application at that filing office - OJ2025 A33
- Thus, the Munich office received EP-A after the expiry of the 12m priority period under A87(1).

On the issue of novelty:
- the oral lecture thus, in principle, becomes A54(2) EPC prior art for EP-A, whose priority claim is invalid. - GL/EPO G-IV, 7.3.1
- It will form part of prior art if date and content can be ascertained - T 406/92
- we assume the lecture was given to people skilled in the art to put the teaching into practice [the content of the lecture was enabling] -  T 877/90.
- although the standard for proving what was disclosed orally is higher than for written disclosures, the fact that it is a "congress" implies at least that there were many people present and there could, in principle, be many witnesses to what was exactly disclosed and some people there understood the presentation - T 877/90
- there were no NDAs or other secrecy agreements for A55(1) EPC regarding non-prejudicial disclosures to apply.
- for all intents and purposes we need to assume the lecture is full A54(2) EPC prior art.

On the issue of recovering priority for EP-A:
- Had priority to CZ-A been validly claimed, the lecture would not be A54(2) prior art for EP-A because CZ-A had been filed before the date of the lecture - A89 EPC
- FP excluded for period of A87(1) - A121(4) EPC
- RE is available if all due care can be proven - A122(1) EPC; R136(3) EPC
- In principle, the RE request needs to be filed within 2m of expiry of the priority period - R136(1) EPC
- In this case it would be: 
- 6 JAN 2026 [Sunday] -> R134(1) EPC -> 7 JAN 2026 + 2m [R131(4) EPC] -> 7 MAR 2026 [Saturday] -> R134(1) EPC -> 9 MAR 2026 [Monday]. 
- So today there is still time to complete the omitted act - R136(2) EPC [already done by filing EP-A] and to pay the RE fee - A2(1).13 RFeesEPC, R136(1) EPC.
- However, standard for proving due care is high at the EPO, and the fact that Mr Pivo merely waited till the very last day and then filed at Vienna will likely not be considered good enough. Misinterpreting the EPC is not sufficient to meet the due care criterion - D 6/82. No other info is given regarding sickness, etc., so right of priority will likely not be reestablished.
- An alternative solution could be to file the ENG contents of EP-A as an international application and request restoration of right of priority under R26bis.3(a) PCT or make use of the 12m grace period in the US.

Question 2
C7-11 (EQE - 9 points) 

The applicant files an application for a German national patent DE. Three months later he files a European patent application EP1 for the same invention as in DE together with a further embodiment, claiming the priority of the earlier German national patent application. The applicant files a second - subsequent - European patent application EP2 claiming patent protection for the subject-matter disclosed in the first European patent application EP1 together with another further embodiment of the invention. 
May priority be validly claimed for this second European patent application EP2, if this application: 
(a)  is filed six months after the German national patent application DE, claiming the priority of both the national application and the European application; 
(b)  is filed six months after the European patent application EP1 and claiming only the priority of the European patent application EP1; 
(c)  is filed eleven months after the European patent application EP1 claiming the priority of the European patent application EP1, the German national application DE having been abandoned before the filing of EP1? 

Timeline:
- DE filed for sjm A.
- 3m later: EP1 filed for DE content + Embodiment A. Call this sjm B. Prio claimed to DE.
- EP2 filed after EP1 for EP1 content + Embodiment B. Call this sjm C.

- General requirements for valid prio claim under A87:
- Same applicant - always fulfilled here
- Paris Convention state (or WTO member) - always fulfilled here
- Same invention - need to analyze
- First application for that invention - need to analyze
- within 12m of date of filing of priority application - need to analyze

(a) EP2 filed 6m after DE; claiming prio to both DE and EP1:
- multiple priorities may be claimed under A88(2) and A88(3) EPC
- 6m after DE is within 12m period of DE and 12m period of EP1 under A87(1) EPC.
- DE was the first app for A. EP1 is first app for B. EP2 is first app for C. Hence, EP2 can claim priority from both DE and EP1 because neither DE nor EP1 is the first application for C. 
- Hence EP2 is first app for C under A87(1).
- Both priorities valid for EP2. 

(b) EP2 is filed 6m after EP1 and claims only prio of EP1:
- within 12m of EP1 (and also within 12m of DE) - OK under A87(1) EPC.
- However, EP1 is not the first application for sjm A. There are rights outstanding to sjm A stemming from DE - A87(4) EPC.
- Hence, prio would at best be conferred for Embodiment 1 in that case for EP2 from EP1 and sjm A would become A54(2) EPC from DE once published. 
- Moreover, DE has already been used to claim priority by EP1. So it cannot be chained for another prio claim by EP2. 
- but we can amend the priority claim of EP2 within 16m of filing date of DE - R52(2) EPC by adding a priority declaration to DE.
- we need to do this no later than 4m from the date of filing of EP2 or no later than 16m from the date of filing of DE - whichever expires later - - R52(3)

(c) DE is abandoned before EP1 is filed. EP2 is filed 11m after EP1. EP2 claims prio to EP1:
- DE was filed 14m before EP2.
- although DE was abandoned before EP1 filing date and has not been published, it leaves rights outstanding because it had already been used to claim priority by EP1. Hence, under A87(4) EPC, EP1 cannot be treated as the first application.
- Moreover, a priority declaration to DE may no longer be added under R52(2) EPC because EP2 was filed more than 12m after the filing date of DE.

Question 3
C7-15 (Selection)	(6 points)

An applicant filed a first patent application SE1 describing and claiming a mosquito repellent comprising a combination of compounds C and K, which causes the mosquito repellent to very efficiently keep mosquitos at a distance. After further experimentation, the applicant found that the mosquito repellent is also very efficient in keep mosquitos at a distance if instead of K, a compound L is used. The applicant compiled a new European application EP2 by taking the full description of SE1, adding the newly found mosquito repellent comprising a combination of compounds and L to the description, and claiming a mosquito repellent comprising a combination of compounds C and at least one of K and L. The applicant duly filed the new European application EP2 at the end of the priority period, while claiming priority from SE1.
A few months after filing SE1, the applicant handed out samples of his mosquito repellent comprising a combination of compounds C and K at an outdoor event in Sweden.
Is the hand-out of the samples at the outdoor event in Sweden novelty-destroying for the claim of EP2?

Timeline:
- SE1 filed for [C and K].
- Outdoor sample hand-out of [C and K] samples a few months later.
- EP2 duly filed claiming prio to SE1 claiming [(C and K) or (C and L) or (C and K and L)].

What is EP2 claiming?
- [C and K] or [C and L] or [C and K and L] ; call these A and B and C, respectively.

Partial priority issue:
- SE1 is the first application for A. It was filed before the outdoor hand-out of A. So hand-out is not novelty destroying A54(2) EPC event for SE1 with respect to sjm A.
- Simiarly, EP2 claims valid prio to SE1 that includes sjm A. Hence, prio for sjm A is secured against the intervening hand-out event and the claim of EP2 directed to A is going to be novel in view of the handout.
- However, sjm B is claimed in EP2 whose priority is not secured by SE1 as SE1 does not disclose B.
- Handout anticipates A only, which is protected by SE1. 
- Handout does not anticipate B. B will be novel against the handout event.
- Claim is a true alternative "OR" claim. At least one of the alternatives needs to have been disclosed for the entire claim to be anticipated. Each alternative needs its own assessment procedure regarding priority - G 2/98
- As neither alternative is disclosed in the handout event, the event was not novelty destroying for the claim of EP2.

Question 4
C7-17 (Selection) 	(7 points) 

Claim 1 of the set of claims of a European application EP-2 reads:

"1. A dosage form comprising a core having an exterior surface and first and second ends; a subcoating over portions of the exterior surface of the core; a first gelatinous coating over at least part of the subcoating; and a second gelatinous coating over at least part of the subcoating; [...], wherein said first and second gelatinous coatings form a gap through which the subcoating is exposed, the gap being from 3,0% to 33% of the length of the elongated tablet as measured along its longest axis; and [...]". 

EP-2 claims priority from an earlier US application US-1 from the same applicant. The same subject-matter as claimed in EP-2 is disclosed in the priority document US-1, but specifying a range of 5,0% to 33%. No examples with gaps smaller than 5,0% of the length of the elongated tables are disclosed in US-1. 

A public disclosure D occurred in the priority interval according to the same kind of gelatinous coated dosage forms wherein the gap has a width which is approximately 17% of the length.

a) Discuss the priority situation and the effect of the public disclosure D on the novelty of the claim.
b) Discuss the priority situation and the effect of the public disclosure D on the novelty of the claim if the public disclosure D would have shown a gap having a width which is 4,0% of the length. 

Timeline:
- US1 from same Applicant discloses range 5–33;
- Public disclosure D discloses approximately 17;
- EP2 from same Applicant claims prio to US1 and discloses 3–33.

a) Priority situation:
- EP2 can claim priority to US1 because USA is a PCT state and is, presumably, the first application for this invention from this Applicant - A4C PC; A87(1) EPC.
- Priority needs to be claimed within 12m of DoF of US1 - A87(1) and a declaration or priority needs to filed within 16m of the DoF of US1 - A88(1) EPC; R 52(2) EPC
- We treat sjm of EP2 as "5–33" and "not 5–33", i.e., in this case, "3–4". Call these A and B, respectively. - G 1/15.
- EP2 enjoys prio to range A based on US1. - A89 EPC. 
- The public disclosure D of a value within that range will not make up state of the art under A54(1); A54(2) EPC.
- Range B is not disclosed by disclosure D. So Range B is also novel over D. 
- Effective filing date of A is DoF of US1. Effective filing date of B is DoF of EP2. Both will not be anticipated by D for the purposes of A54 EPC.

b) Priority situation:
- EP2 can claim priority to US1 because USA is a PCT state and is, presumably, the first application for this invention from this Applicant - A4C PC; A87(1) EPC.
- Priority needs to be claimed within 12m of DoF of US1 - A87(1) and a declaration or priority needs to filed within 16m of the DoF of US1 - A88(1) EPC; R 52(2) EPC
- We treat sjm of EP2 as "5–33" and "not 5–33", i.e., in this case, "3–4". Call these A and B, respectively.
- EP2 enjoys prio to range A based on US1. The public disclosure D of a value within that range will not make up state of the art under A54(1); A54(2) EPC.
- Range B IS disclosed by disclosure D. So Range B is not novel over D. 
- Effective filing date of A is DoF of US1. Effective filing date of B is DoF of EP2. Range B is anticipated by D for the purposes of A54 EPC.
- An undisclosed disclaimer may not be used against an A54(2) EPC attack.
- Unless the description has more granular ranges and/or separation from A and B that could be used to amend claim 1 of EP2, the whole range of EP2 is spoiled by D and the entire claim falls on A54(2) EPC.
